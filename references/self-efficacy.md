# Self-efficacy notes: why belief and hard goals matter, for agents too

The essay closes on a psychological point. People are held back by what they believe is possible, and usually trust themselves only inside a narrow discipline. Self-efficacy (Bandura) is that belief in one's own capability, and it predicts accomplishment. The essay suggests it can be "hacked".

| Lever | Human evidence (from the essay) | Agent translation |
|---|---|---|
| Specific hard goals beat "do your best" | Locke and Latham's review of about 35 years of goal-setting research | Step 5: a numeric, impossible-sounding target, with the literal ask as the floor |
| Believing it is possible | Dantzig solved two open problems he thought were homework | Treat the target as solvable while working. Do not open with reasons it cannot be done |
| Seeing someone else do it | After Bannister's four-minute mile, others followed quickly | Look for prior art: someone has hit a similar bar. Cite it in the plan to anchor the target |
| Permission to fail | *The Inner Game of Tennis*; Linux "just a hobby" | A missed hard target, honestly reported with real numbers, is an acceptable outcome. A quietly lowered goal is not |
| Narrow self-trust (Michelangelo: "I am a sculptor") | | Do not refuse a method because it is outside the obvious discipline. That is what Range is for |

## Guardrails
- Belief is about effort and search, not about claims. Confidence never replaces the judge.
- Persistence has a stopping rule: at least two genuinely different approaches, each measured, then report the concrete blocker.
- Ambition never overrides gates, safety or explicit user scope.
